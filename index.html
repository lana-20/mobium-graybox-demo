A screen can't say the work is done.

A test sees what a person sees. The app knows what it is doing. Two ways to decide when to tap:

watch the screen

black box: on screen, still, enabled, uncovered

ask the app

gray box: busy, or idle and on screen

Black box is the right default and stays the default: it needs nothing from the app. Gray box is for the work a screen hides, and it needs the app to take part. This is the thesis; everything after is how, and how we know.

02

Ask the app

The app says when it is busy. Mobium listens on channels it already holds. One flag turns it on.

Section divider. Two seconds on screen. Do not read it aloud.

Two calls in the app: busy, and idle once it shows

const refresh = () => {

  GrayBox.busy('quiet');

  // ... the work; when it is done, the new rows ...

};

 

useEffect(() => {

  GrayBox.idle('quiet');  // after the new rows render

}, [generation]);

Idle means done and on screen, not "the response arrived". Only the app can say that, so the app decides what counts as work.

Simplified from MobiumApp's Busy Demo (App.tsx): the real code counts pending work and calls idle in an effect that runs after the commit. GrayBox is Mobium's gray-box library, a local Expo module in MobiumApp, modules/graybox: about ninety lines of Swift and seventy of Kotlin. A native app writes the same lines itself.

What the app writes to the device log

linemeaning
MOBIUM-GRAYBOX onthe library is listening
MOBIUM-GRAYBOX busy=1 tag=quietwork started; one thing in flight
MOBIUM-GRAYBOX still busy=1every half second while it is
MOBIUM-GRAYBOX busy=0 tag=quietfinished, and on screen
MOBIUM-GRAYBOX lifta finger came up
MOBIUM-GRAYBOX away / backthe app left the foreground / returned

Silent unless the app was launched with the gray box on: -MobiumGrayBox YES on iOS, the intent extra MobiumGrayBox=true on Android. A person running the same build never turns it on.

The whole contract is these lines: an os_log subsystem, dev.mobium.graybox, on iOS, at the notice level with values public; logcat, tag MobiumGrayBox, on Android. iOS keeps a launch argument for that launch only; a launch from the home screen carries no intent extra. So the build you test is the build you could hand anyone, and nothing in it talks unless the test asked.

How the lines reach Mobium: nothing new listens

devicechannelmeasured
iPhonethe log stream a session already holds1–4 ms
simulatora log stream narrowed to the subsystemlive
Androidlogcat, from the device's clock at launchlive, gap-proof

No server in the app, and no port opened on anyone's phone. The way out is the device log, which a test already reads.

1 to 4 ms: on the iPhone 15 Plus, from the app writing a line to Mobium reading it over syslog_relay, with the phone's clock read through lockdown before and after the run to take out the offset (it was 37 to 38 ms ahead of the Mac). Android reads logcat from the device's own time at launch, so a line from an earlier launch is never read, and a restart picks up from the last line's time, so nothing in a gap is lost.

test file

  "app": "dev.mobium.mobiumapp",

The same in MCP and every client — JavaScript, Go, Java, .NET. The steps don't change, and no sleep is added.

app_launch with gray_box: true over MCP; launch(app, { grayBox: true }) in JavaScript; LaunchWithGrayBox in Go and .NET; launchWithGrayBox in Java. In a mobium test file, "grayBox": true beside "app" makes the launch before every test turn it on. An app without the library launches normally and the result says it did not answer, so nothing is waited for by mistake.

03

A door that closes

What a wait does, and what happens when the app can't answer: crashed, backgrounded, never finished, or not heard.

Section divider. Two seconds on screen. Do not read it aloud.

Before every touch, three things in order

1  the lift

the last tap's finger-up has been heard

2  150 ms of grace

for work that tap starts to be announced

3  busy=0

nothing in flight, then find the target

0–15 ms

from the lift to the app's busy line, 200 trials

~35 ms

what a wait costs when the app is idle

The wait runs before the target is resolved, not just before the touch, so the target is found on the screen the work produced. The grace is ten times the worst gap measured: in 200 trials on the iPhone the app's busy line came 0 to 15 ms after the lift. With no lift heard during an action, as when the button is in an alert's own window, the grace counts from the action's end instead.

Busy is a lease, not a promise

While work is in flight the app restates it every half second, from a native timer. Unrenewed for 1.5 s, it is stale: the app crashed holding it, or was suspended.

Crash during a refresh, then a tap  ·  emulator

$ mobium tap 300 30

tapped (300, 30)

gray box: not waited — the app stopped saying it is busy 3s ago (it was busy with doomed)

Without the lease, that tap would have waited 10 s for a dead app, then failed.

The renewal runs on a native timer, not on the JavaScript thread, so an app that is busy in JavaScript still renews, and a dead or suspended process stops. The output is verbatim from the Pixel 7 emulator run's transcript: Crash during a refresh holds a busy lease named doomed and dies 300 ms in; the next tap, on the status bar, is not held up. On Android the app often says away on its way down, and then that is the reason given: the same answer, sooner.

Each row is a row of docs/checks/graybox-edges.sh in Mobium, driven by a button on MobiumApp's Busy Demo: Refresh from an alert, Refresh twice, Keep polling, Crash during a refresh. Home and the crash leave the app, so on a phone they run only when the owner says so (ALLOW_PHONE=1): they change no setting and record nothing outside MobiumApp, and they passed on the Pixel 8 Pro and the iPhone 15 Plus. The killed stream: on Android it is adb logcat on the Mac and on a simulator a log stream, killed from outside; on a real iPhone it is a connection inside Mobium's daemon, which the daemon drops on SIGUSR1 the way an unplugged cable would. Aimed so the work begins inside the gap, that drop found a real defect: the reconnect trusted a count of zero and the tap landed stale twice in three. Now a reconnect waits 700 ms for the app to restate its work: three in three current (CHALLENGES 252, mobium #134). All four devices pass all six rows.

Proving it

A fix has nothing to prove until the problem has shown up in the same run. And the recorder can change what it records.

The check fails if the normal launch never taps a stale row. A zero is not a result until the same setup has shown it can come back non-zero.

Three columns because three questions: does the race happen (normal), does auto-wait already handle what it can see (the spinner, the control: never stale), and does the gray box close the gap. Each row is graded by the app's own outcome line, never by what the tool reported.

Watching changed the timing

The first Pixel recording showed no race at all. A full trace screenshots and reads the screen after every call, and every call waits for it.

no trace

1.4 s

5.1 s

trace, no screens

1.3 s

With the work lasting at most 1.6 s, five seconds per two taps let it finish first, every time. The demo records the screen as video and traces calls without screens.

Two taps, Refresh quietly then Row B, timed on the Pixel 8 Pro: 1.59 and 1.35 s untraced, 5.12 and 5.42 s with a full trace, 1.35 and 1.30 s with trace --no-screenshots --no-maps. The bars are drawn to that scale, at 200 px a second. The first Pixel run, with full traces, landed current five times in five. Mobium's trace help now says so. The general lesson: any instrument that adds time to each step can make a race disappear.

Five habits to take home

1

Declare work at both ends, and call it done only once it is on screen.

2

Keep the hook silent unless a test turned it on.

3

Treat busy as a lease, so a dead app never holds a test hostage.

4

Make the black box fail first, in the same run, before trusting the fix.

5

Record a race with something that adds no time to each step.

Each habit is a slide of the talk in one line: the two calls, the silent-by-default library, the lease, the control, and the recorder. None of them needs Mobium in particular; they are what any gray-box hook should do.

Ask  ·  wait  ·  prove

Don't guess when the app is done. Ask it, wait for its word, and make the test say what it waited for.

The screen stays the default, and needs nothing from the app. For the work a screen hides, two calls in the app and one key in the test turn a flaky tap into a steady one.
